In the lessons, every displayed formula is followed by a list that says what each letter in it means. This page gathers those lists in one place: {{N_SYMBOLS}} entries from the 18 modules, sorted by letter, Latin letters first and Greek letters after them. It is built from the lessons themselves each time the site is built, so it always agrees with them.

How to use it:

- **Type in the box** to filter. Greek letters are found by their name: type `lambda`, `sigma` or `tau`. A subscript is typed with an underscore: `k_B`, `N_df`, `r_ij`. You can also type a word of the meaning, such as `friction` or `free energy`.
- **The same letter can mean different things in different modules.** There are more quantities than letters, so $\lambda$ is a coupling parameter in Module 13, a Lagrange multiplier in Module 9 and an eigenvalue in Module 16. Each module has one row per symbol, with its first definition there. Look at the last column to see which module a meaning belongs to, and follow the link to read the formula it comes from.
- A quantity written with $\Delta$ in front, such as $\Delta G$, is filed under its letter, here G. Derivatives such as $\partial U/\partial x$ are filed under the quantity that is differentiated.

For how formulas are written in general (bold letters, dots, sums, brackets), see [How to read a formula](maths.html#notation) in the maths toolbox. The equations themselves are collected on the [formula sheet](formula-sheet.html).
