  • Alder BJ; Wainwright TE (1957). Phase Transition for a Hard Sphere System. The Journal of Chemical Physics 27, 1208-1209. read in fullTwo-page letter reporting the first event-driven molecular dynamics equation of state for 32 and 108 hard spheres, with two pressure branches that strongly suggested a first-order fluid-solid transition.What to read in it: The whole letter: the paragraph on the two branches and why 32 particles cannot hold two phases. For Module 1 (history) and Module 7 (periodic boundaries).
  • Alder BJ; Wainwright TE (1959). Studies in Molecular Dynamics. I. General Method. The Journal of Chemical Physics 31, 459-466. read in fullThe founding methods paper of molecular dynamics: event-driven exact collisions for hard-sphere and square-well particles, periodic boundaries, cost and size limits, and the case for dynamics over Monte Carlo.What to read in it: Limitations (periodic boundaries, 1/N error) and Description of the method (collision cycles). For Module 1 (history) and Module 7 (boundaries, neighbour search).
  • Rahman A (1964). Correlations in the Motion of Atoms in Liquid Argon. Physical Review 136, A405-A411. read in fullFirst simulation of liquid argon with a continuous Lennard-Jones potential on 864 atoms: g(r) and diffusion close to experiment, a negative velocity autocorrelation, and a better Van Hove approximation.What to read in it: Section II (model numbers), Section III (g(r), D, velocity autocorrelation) and the Appendix (reduced equations of motion, predictor-corrector). For Module 1 and Module 4.
  • Verlet L (1967). Computer "Experiments" on Classical Fluids. I. Thermodynamical Properties of Lennard-Jones Molecules. Physical Review 159, 98-103. read in full864 Lennard-Jones particles integrated with the simple position recursion and a periodically rebuilt neighbour table that saved a factor of ten, showing that argon's equilibrium state follows from a two-body potential.What to read in it: Section II (units, Eq. 3 to 7: recursion, velocity formula, neighbour table and its condition) and Section III A to D (temperature, Eq. 8 pressure, comparison with argon). For Module 4 and Module 7.
